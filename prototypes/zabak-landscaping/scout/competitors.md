## Notes

**The "Design | Build | Maintain" pattern is the dominant category framing.** Both Turf World and DiCillo use this exact structure in their hero. Zabak's site should differentiate by leading with the personal story and the 35-year word-of-mouth reputation rather than competing on service category language.

**No competitor shows a before/after gallery prominently.** This is the single biggest gap across all three. A dedicated before/after section would immediately differentiate Zabak's prototype and give visitors the most persuasive visual evidence of quality.

**Heritage messaging is contested but winnable.** DiCillo's 100-year story is unbeatable on raw years, but their website doesn't tell it emotionally — it's stated as fact without warmth. Zabak's Tavern & Restaurant backstory (grandparents, neighborhood roots, built from a lawnmower) is more human and more relatable than a corporate century of operation. Lead with the story.

**Review counts are universally weak on competitor sites.** None of the three display live Google or Yelp star ratings on their homepage. Embedding a live review widget or prominently displaying "5.0 on Angi" and the BBB A+ badge would out-trust all three competitors from day one.

**Free consultation is not prominently offered by competitors.** Zabak's practice of free consultations, mentioned in his press coverage, should be a headline feature — competitors either don't offer it or don't advertise it.
